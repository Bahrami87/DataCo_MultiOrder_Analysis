# STAR Case Study — DataCo Profit Analysis

> **Use this document in interviews.** It structures the project as a Situation–Task–Action–Result narrative — the format most interviewers expect for portfolio projects.

---

## 🔵 Situation

DataCo is an international retail chain operating across five global markets: Africa, Europe, LATAM, Pacific Asia, and USCA.

The company holds transactional data at the order-item level covering 65,752 orders and over 180,000 line items. Initial examination revealed that the business was generating negative total profit despite high revenue — a clear signal of structural inefficiency.

**Key contextual facts:**
- 37.5% of all orders are loss-making
- The company's net profit across all markets is **−£1.75 million**
- All five markets individually are in the red

The business had no clear diagnosis of *why* orders were losing money, and no data-driven basis for corrective action.

---

## 🎯 Task

My objective was to conduct a structured SQL analysis to:

1. Identify where profit is being destroyed
2. Test and eliminate competing hypotheses (discounts, delivery, geography)
3. Pinpoint the root cause with evidence
4. Translate findings into actionable business recommendations

This project reflects the work of a data analyst embedded in a commercial team — not an academic exercise.

---

## ⚙️ Action

### Step 1 — Data Audit
Before any analysis, I validated the dataset structure. I found that `Order_Profit_Per_Order` repeats across all items in an order. Summing it directly would inflate profit by the number of items. I documented this and built all analysis to correct for it.

### Step 2 — Restructure into Analytical Layers
I created three SQL views to separate concerns:

| View | Level | Purpose |
|------|-------|---------|
| `v_order` | Order | Profit, delivery, item count per order |
| `v_product` | Product/Category | Sales, discount, margin per category |
| `v_item_profit` | Allocated | Proportional profit per item (avoids double-counting) |

### Step 3 — KPI Baseline
Using `v_order`, I established the KPI baseline:
- Net profit: **−£1.75M**
- Loss-making orders: **37.5%**
- On-time delivery: **45.2%**
- Average delay: **+0.57 days**
- Average discount: **10.2%**

### Step 4 — Hypothesis Testing

I tested three explanations for the losses:

**H1 — Discounts are the cause**
Compared average discount rates between loss and profit orders. The difference was less than 0.1 percentage points. *Rejected.*

**H2 — Late delivery is the cause**
Compared late delivery rates between multi-item and single-item orders. Rates were statistically identical (~54.7% vs ~55.1%). *Rejected.*

**H3 — Certain categories are the cause**
Using allocated profit (proportional by item sales share), I found that several categories — Fishing (−£418K), Cleats (−£251K), Camping & Hiking (−£240K) — are heavily loss-making. *Partially confirmed.* But item-level margins were still positive, meaning something at the order level was absorbing profit.

### Step 5 — Root Cause Analysis: Multi-Item Orders

This was the pivotal discovery. I segmented all orders by item count and found:

| Items per Order | Avg Profit |
|-----------------|-----------|
| 1 | **+£27.46** |
| 2 | −£17.97 |
| 3 | −£42.71 |
| 4 | −£60.11 |
| 5 | **−£79.21** |

The relationship is **nearly perfectly linear**: each additional item reduces profit by ~£20–22.

Revenue, by contrast, scales linearly and healthily (~+£240 per item). Only profit collapses.

Since discounts and late delivery are flat across item counts, the only explanation consistent with all the evidence is an **unmodelled per-item fulfilment cost** — picking, packing, and handling costs that grow with item count but are not recovered through pricing.

I confirmed this pattern holds across **all five markets** — ruling out any regional or operational cause.

### Step 6 — Business Impact Quantification

| Segment | Orders | % of Orders | Revenue | % of Revenue | Profit |
|---------|--------|-------------|---------|--------------|--------|
| Multi-Item | 45,902 | 70% | £32.0M | 87% | **−£2.30M** |
| Single-Item | 19,850 | 30% | £4.7M | 13% | **+£545K** |

**87% of DataCo's revenue is generated by a segment that loses money on every single order.**

---

## 📈 Result

### Findings Delivered

1. **Root cause identified**: Multi-item order losses are structural and universal — not driven by discounts, delivery failures, or regional issues.
2. **Profit erosion quantified**: Multi-item orders lose £50 per order on average; single-item orders earn £27.
3. **Business risk articulated**: The company is growing revenue in a segment that systematically destroys value.

### Recommendations Made

| Action | Expected Impact |
|--------|----------------|
| Introduce a per-item handling fee for orders with 2+ items | Recovers £15–20 of unmodelled fulfilment cost per additional item |
| Set a minimum profit threshold — reject or flag orders that fall below it | Reduces the 37.5% loss-making order rate |
| Prioritise high-margin products in multi-item cart recommendations | Reduces structural losses without cutting order volume |
| Audit fulfilment costs per SKU for top loss categories | Enables targeted pricing corrections |

### Skills Demonstrated

- SQL view design for multi-level analytical restructuring
- Hypothesis-driven analysis (test and eliminate, not assume)
- Correct handling of order-item level data (avoiding double-counting)
- Translating statistical findings into business language
- Stakeholder-ready quantification of financial impact
